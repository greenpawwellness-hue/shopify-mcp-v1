Green Paw Wellness · Cat-first brand concept

Cats Hide It

The one brand truth a dog company structurally cannot use — and the line that gets built on top of it.

The premise

A prey animal doesn’t show weakness

Cats mask illness and pain as a survival instinct. In the wild, visible weakness marks you as the one worth chasing, and domestic cats never lost the wiring. Every veterinarian says the same thing: by the time an owner notices something is wrong, it has usually been developing for weeks or months.

That is a fact about cats, not a fact about supplements — which is exactly why it can carry a brand. It’s emotionally true to anyone who has ever owned a cat, it is not available to a dog company, and no dogs-and-cats brand will ever lead with it, because half their customers would find it irrelevant.

Your cat is not fine. Your cat is a professional at looking fine.

The proof

The gap is enormous and nobody talks about it

Feline degenerative joint disease is the cleanest demonstration of the premise, because the prevalence is settled and the detection rate is catastrophic.

And the cat barely gets the chance to be looked at. In 2024, 74.2% of dog owners made at least one veterinary visit; only 57.3% of cat owners did. Around 40% of cats don’t see a vet in a given year against 15% of dogs, and the cats who do go average 26% fewer visits. Owners read quietness as low maintenance.

Why this is the strongest daily-supplement argument in the category

The multivitamin category’s core weakness is that a healthy pet on complete food doesn’t need one. For cats that objection inverts: the animal conceals problems and is seen by a professional half as often, so the daily bowl is where care actually happens. This is the only honest version of “start before symptoms” anyone in pet wellness can make — and it only works for cats.

Claim guardrail

Cite prevalence in education, never in a product claim. A supplement supports joint health; it does not treat, prevent, or manage arthritis — that’s a drug claim and it’s the fastest way to get a warning letter. The statistic belongs in your content, the structure/function language belongs on the label, and the two must never be joined in a single sentence.

The product

Not the dog formula, smaller

A demographic wedge only holds if the product genuinely changes. For cats, three things do.

Nutrients they physically cannot make

Cats require dietary taurine, preformed vitamin A (no carotenoid conversion), arachidonic acid, and preformed niacin (no tryptophan conversion), plus more arginine and B6. A dog manufactures all of it internally. This is the difference that makes “cats are not small dogs” a formulation fact rather than a slogan.

Palatability is a formulation problem, not a flavor

This is the part most brands get wrong. A cat has roughly 470 taste buds against a dog’s 1,700 and a human’s ~10,000, and it cannot taste sweetness at all — the Tas1r2 sweet-receptor gene is a pseudogene in cats. They’re driven by umami and free amino acids, and they’re neophobic by design, since refusing unfamiliar food is a decent anti-poisoning strategy.

So every palatability trick the dog category runs on — sweeteners, molasses binders, bacon notes, the soft-chew format itself — is literally wasted on a cat. What works is protein hydrolysates and yeast extracts. A brand that says this out loud sounds like it knows something, because it does.

Dose size is where the packet finally earns its cost

An 8–12 lb animal takes a fraction of a dog’s dose. At that size a scoop stops being approximate and starts being wrong, and the margin for error on fat-soluble vitamins narrows. This is the one place single-serve packaging is doing real work rather than adding cost — the opposite of the dental-powder case.

And the conditions are different

Urinary and FLUTD, hairballs, kidney health, dental, weight, thyroid, and hidden mobility loss. Not hips and joints as a dog brand means it. A cat line built on dog categories announces itself immediately.

The distinction

Squeezables is a product. This is a company.

Worth being precise, because this is the thing that separates the wedge from a line extension. Pet Honesty added an excellent cat product — eight lickable purées, eighteen months of palatability work. But their homepage leads with dogs, cat is a tab, and the cat range is largely their dog conditions ported across with hairball and urinary added. Their stated claim is about format: wellness in a format cats already love.

Nobody gets to be the cat brand while their revenue, their homepage, and their category structure are canine.

The identity

Where the visual white space actually is

Cat branding today lives in three places, and all three are taken or worthless.

KitschPaw prints, pastels, pun names, the knowing cat-lady wink. Treats the owner as a joke.
ClinicalWhite, blue, veterinary. Credible and completely cold.
Borrowed dogEarthy, outdoorsy, adventure, trail greens. Coded for an animal that goes outside.
UnclaimedAdult, quiet, domestic, observational. Indoors, at night, in routine and small movements.

The last one comes straight out of the premise. If cats hide it, then the entire job of a cat owner is noticing — and no other pet category can say that. A brand built on attention rather than enthusiasm: restrained type, low light, interiors, close observation, an adult tone that assumes the reader is paying attention because they already are. That’s a design direction derived from the animal, which is why it won’t look like everyone else’s.

It is also the exact structural move Kelly White made with rhinestones and leopard print — take the customer the category treats as secondary and build the whole aesthetic for them. The execution is nothing alike. The mechanic is identical.

The line

What you’d actually sell

Hero

The Daily

Built on the four nutrients a cat can’t synthesize. This SKU exists to state the premise — it is the product that proves you are a cat company, and it anchors the subscription.

Engine

Mobility

Where the 92%-present, 1%-detected statistic points. The education acquires; this converts. Second SKU, but likely your best-performing paid creative.

Native

Urinary · Hairball

The two conditions with no dog equivalent and real search demand. They confirm the brand is feline by construction, not by relabel.

Later

Dental

85% of cats aged three-plus have dental disease and the crowded powder field is almost entirely dog-first. Park it until a VOHC trial is fundable — then it’s a moat.

Note what isn’t here: calming, skin and coat, allergy, parasite, greens. Those are dog categories with cat labels, which is precisely the catalog this brand exists to argue against.

The cost

What you give up

Eight SKUs of current revenue can’t share a homepage with a cat-first brand — the whole proposition dies the moment a visitor sees a dog. Two workable routes: sunset the dog line slowly while the cat brand takes investment, or run it as a separate value storefront under its own name and let it fund the transition. What doesn’t work is one site trying to hold both stories.

The name survives, at least. A paw is a paw, and Green Paw reads as easily feline as canine — so this is a repositioning rather than a renaming, which is meaningfully cheaper. Whether you want a species-neutral name for a species-specific brand is a real question, and it’s yours.

Before committing

Pull your cat revenue share. The market baseline is ~31%. Above it, your own customers have already validated the wedge and the rebrand starts with momentum. Well below it, you’re starting from zero — still viable, but a different and slower plan than this document assumes.
